                Feels like

                Wet bulb globe temperature, which counts sun, wind and humidity as well as heat, is measured by NEA in Singapore only. Feels like is the nearest thing everywhere else.

                Forecasts from Open-Meteo, with the national weather service laid over them where there is one: in Singapore, readings, forecasts, radar and lightning from NEA via data.gov.sg; in the US, the National Weather Service; in Norway, MET Norway and NILU (CC BY 4.0); in Canada, Environment and Climate Change Canada; in Germany, the Deutscher Wetterdienst (CC BY 4.0) via Bright Sky; in England, rain gauges from the Environment Agency (contains public sector information licensed under the Open Government Licence v3.0); in Ireland, Met Éireann (copyright Met Éireann, CC BY 4.0; Met Éireann does not accept any liability whatsoever for any error or omission in the data, their availability, or for any loss or damage arising from their use); in Switzerland, source: MeteoSwiss, its stations and its ICON-CH model via Open-Meteo; across Europe, station readings and MeteoAlarm's warnings from EUMETNET's members via MeteoGate (CC BY 4.0); in China, Seniverse, and air quality from China's national monitoring network via the World Air Quality Index Project. When Open-Meteo is down, WeatherAPI.com, OpenWeather or Xweather stand in. Radar elsewhere from RainViewer. The map from OpenFreeMap (OpenMapTiles, data from OpenStreetMap contributors), drawn with MapLibre, and OpenStreetMap's own tiles when OpenFreeMap is down.
